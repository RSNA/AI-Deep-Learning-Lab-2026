## Overview

This hands-on workshop provides a practical, clinically grounded framework for evaluating deep learning models for real-world deployment in radiology. The session shifts the focus from traditional research metrics to deployment-specific considerations, emphasizing how models behave under real-world conditions such as data shift, data drift, and model drift. Through an example-based approach, participants will learn how deployment-focused evaluation differs from conventional research evaluation, with emphasis on generalizability, uncertainty, clinical utility, and post-deployment considerations.

The hands-on portion is delivered through a guided, pre-configured notebook that lets participants explore real deployment scenarios using representative medical imaging models and datasets, without requiring extensive programming experience. Attendees will examine model performance under changing data distributions, review which evaluation and monitoring metrics to use and when, visualize calibration and uncertainty, and learn how uncertainty quantification can flag distributional changes and potential model degradation over time. The course also covers human-in-the-loop strategies, showing how radiologist oversight and feedback support safe integration, ongoing monitoring, and continuous improvement of AI systems in clinical practice.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. Define the key criteria that determine whether an AI model is ready for real-world clinical deployment beyond research performance metrics.
2. Distinguish between internal validation performance and true generalizability across diverse clinical settings.
3. Describe frameworks for evaluating uncertainty quantification, safety, and workflow integration of AI tools.
4. Identify common pitfalls and failure modes that arise when transitioning AI models from research to clinical practice.
5. Apply a structured evaluation checklist to assess the deployment readiness of an AI model using a hands-on case scenario.
